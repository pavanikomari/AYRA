Legal Empowerment

Know Your Constitutional & Legal Rights

Knowledge is your strongest shield. Learn your guaranteed legal protections under Indian law, explained with practical steps.

Workplace

Prevention of Sexual Harassment (POSH Act, 2013)

Every employer with 10 or more employees must have an Internal Committee (IC). Women have the right to a confidential, time-bound investigation and protection from retaliation.

90-day time limit to complete inquiry Right to seek transfer or paid leave during inquiry Strict confidentiality of victim identity
Criminal & Police

Right to Zero FIR & Safe Police Investigation

An FIR can be lodged at any police station across India, irrespective of jurisdiction. The station must accept the complaint and subsequently transfer it to the relevant station.

Police cannot refuse registration under any excuse Women cannot be summoned to station after sunset Right to be accompanied by a female officer or lawyer
Family & Home

Protection from Domestic Violence Act (PWDVA, 2005)

Covers physical, emotional, sexual, verbal, and economic abuse. Guarantees the right to reside in the shared household, protection orders, and financial maintenance.

Protection from dispossession or eviction Monthly monetary maintenance and medical compensation Free Protection Officer assistance in every district
Workplace

Maternity Benefit Act (Amendment 2017)

Guarantees 26 weeks (6 months) of paid leave for pregnant working women. Prohibits dismissal during pregnancy and mandates crèche facilities in establishments with 50+ workers.

Full salary payment during the 26 weeks Work-from-home options upon mutual agreement Crèche access visits up to 4 times a day
Property & Finance

Equal Coparcenary Property Inheritance Rights

Following the landmark Supreme Court ruling (2020) under Hindu Succession Act, daughters have an unconditional equal birthright to ancestral property, on par with sons.

Applies regardless of whether father was alive in 2005 Full rights over Stridhan (gifts, jewelry & self-acquired) Equal right to demand partition in ancestral assets
Judicial Access

Right to Free Legal Aid (Section 12, NALSA Act)

Section 12(c) of the Legal Services Authorities Act provides that all women are unconditionally eligible for free government legal services, irrespective of their annual income.

Free court advocate appointed at no expense Exemption from court drafting and filing fees Available from Taluk level to Supreme Court
National Initiatives

Government Schemes & Opportunities

Curated and verified financial, maternal, entrepreneurial and educational support schemes by the Government of India.

Girl Child Welfare Ministry of WCD

Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (BBBP)

National flagship scheme focused on survival, protection, and education of girl children, coupled with high-interest Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) savings accounts.

Interest: Up to 8.2% tax-free under SSY
Eligibility: Girls from birth up to 10 years
Where: Post Offices & National Banks
Entrepreneurship Ministry of Finance

Stand-Up India Scheme

Facilitates bank loans between ₹10 Lakh and ₹1 Crore to at least one woman borrower per bank branch for setting up greenfield manufacturing, service, or trading enterprises.

Funding: ₹10,00,000 to ₹1,00,00,000
Purpose: New greenfield businesses
Repayment: Up to 7 years with moratorium
Maternal Health Ministry of WCD

Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana

Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) cash incentive of ₹5,000 to ₹6,000 directly into the bank accounts of pregnant and lactating mothers for health checkups, nutrition, and institutional delivery.

Benefit: ₹5,000 in DBT instalments
Target: Pregnant & Lactating Mothers
Where: Anganwadi / Health Centres
Digital Commerce Ministry of MSME

Udyam Sakhi & Mahila Co-op Grants

Dedicated portal and financial schemes encouraging women entrepreneurs, Self-Help Groups (SHGs), and micro-enterprises with subsidized loans, training, and direct market linkage.

Support: Zero-fee business registration
Loan Subsidy: Low-interest collateral support
Beneficiaries: Artisans, SHGs, Solopreneurs

Digital Safety & Evidence Checklist

If you or someone you know faces digital harassment or abuse, follow these immediate precautionary steps:

  • Take Screenshots: Preserve URLs, timestamps, handles, and chat context.
  • Do Not Delete Chats: Crucial forensic evidence for Section 66E / 67 IT Act complaints.
  • File at cybercrime.gov.in: Anonymized portal for reporting morphing or deepfakes.
Notice: In an ongoing life-threatening emergency, please dial 112 immediately. AYRA is an informational resource and does not operate an independent police dispatch service.
